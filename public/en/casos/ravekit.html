Diagnosis & Friction

NEIGHBORHOOD BUSINESSES LIVE ON THEIR REVIEWS AND HAVE NO EASY WAY TO ASK FOR THEM

A dentist, a garage or a hair salon depends on how it shows up on Google Maps. Review tools are built for chains: they cost hundreds of dollars a month and are sold through sales calls.

star_half

Enterprise pricing

The well-known platforms are quoted case by case and are out of reach for a small shop.

payments

Asking feels awkward

Asking for a review in person is uncomfortable and gets forgotten in the rush at the counter.

forum

Happy customers stay quiet

The happy customer leaves without a word; the angry one writes on their own.

storefront

Reviews nobody sees

Good reviews stay on Google and never show up on the shop's website.

Product architecture

THREE PILLARS: ASK, PLACE THE QR AND SHOW

PILLAR 01 chat_bubble

Ask in one tap

After the visit, the business sends a WhatsApp or SMS message from its own phone with the link to its Google review form, already written.

Channels WhatsApp · SMS · Automatic follow-ups (coming soon)
PILLAR 02 qr_code_2

QR for the counter

Print-ready templates with the business's brand: acrylic or wooden table tent, cup sticker and mirror decal.

Where it goes Front desk · Counter · Checkout · Every table
PILLAR 03 widgets

Review wall

The business's real reviews on its own website with a single line of code, plus a public page.

Rule No filtering of negative reviews, as Google requires
